Blockchain

Blockchain research and projects

My doctorate at Université Paris Cité was on making blockchain systems scale without giving up trust. The work since then follows the same thread: sharding, cross-chain control, oracle trust and, most recently, what quantum computing does to a transaction while it waits in the mempool. Every project below has a peer-reviewed paper behind it, and the recent ones ship with a reproducible simulator.

Accepted 2026 · Springer

Blockchain-enabled 5G vehicular networks

Secure and scalable design for 5G vehicular networks using federated trust-aware sharding and cross-shard authentication, so vehicles can be authenticated across shards without a central authority and without the ledger becoming the bottleneck.

Cluster Computing (Springer), accepted September 2026 · 10.1007/s10586-026-06593-w · preprint
Accepted 2026 · IEEE BCCA

ASTRA: trustless cross-chain control

Stable and omission-resilient control for cross-chain services: a controller that keeps cross-chain batching stable when relayers drop messages or go quiet, without trusting any single party. Accepted at IEEE BCCA 2026; an extended journal version with a full reproducibility artifact is in preparation.

With Laaziz Lahlou, Farid Naït-Abdesselam and Nadjia Kara
2026 · IEEE ICACT

Q-MEMShield: quantum-resilient mempool

A mempool architecture for the day a quantum computer can derive a private key from a public one during the seconds a transaction spends waiting to be mined. Follow-on work derives exposure bounds for these "on-spend" attacks and shows why blanket post-quantum migration can endanger the accounts that cannot move.

IEEE ICACT 2026, with co-authors at STC Saudi Arabia · journal extension in preparation
2023–2024 · Oracles

Trust in decentralised blockchain oracles

Oracles bring real-world data on chain, and they are the weakest link in most smart-contract systems. This work applies asymmetric Byzantine quorums so that an oracle network tolerates untrusted data sources without a single trusted aggregator.

IEEE SoftCOM 2023, extended to a journal article · arXiv 2401.00175
2023–2025 · Scalability

Sharded and layered blockchain architectures

The PhD line of work: heuristic sharding to load-balance DNS across a blockchain, layered architectures that separate consensus from execution, and optimised load balancing among shards, each measured against throughput and latency rather than asserted.

IEEE BCCA 2023 · IEEE CommNet 2023 · Cluster Computing 2025
